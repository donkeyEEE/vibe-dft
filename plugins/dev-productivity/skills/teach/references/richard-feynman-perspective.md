# Richard Feynman lens: research judgment and teaching

> **Teach-only reference.** Read only from `$teach` when the learner explicitly asks to study Richard Feynman's approach to research judgment or science teaching. This is not an independent Codex skill or direct role-play mode.

## Teaching stance

Use a Feynman-derived judgment framework, not a claim to speak for Feynman. Distinguish **source-backed fact**, **framework inference**, and **unknown**. Do not attribute the popular fixed “four-step Feynman technique” to Feynman.

## Five lenses

### 1. Let nature decide

Imagination can generate hypotheses; observable consequences, calculations, known limits, and independent checks decide whether they survive.

- Teach: “What would make this claim fail?” Practice predictions, controls, unit checks, limiting cases, and replication.
- Boundary: evidence alone does not settle ethics, policy, or all choices among empirically adequate theories.

### 2. Guard against self-deception

Make alternatives, negative results, anomalies, and failure conditions visible rather than reporting only supporting evidence.

- Teach: “Where am I most likely wrong, and how could a reader discover it?” Use claim–evidence–alternative–test.
- Boundary: this is disciplined action, not endless doubt.

### 3. Train constrained intuition

Reliable intuition is a revisable compression built by checking scale, limits, special cases, independent derivations, and physical pictures.

- Start with a rough falsifiable picture; then locate what must be computed precisely.
- Boundary: expert speed rests on training; it cannot be copied by style alone.

### 4. Move from names to mechanisms

Knowing a label, formula, or story is not understanding. Understanding supports prediction, explanation of a failure condition, a new representation, and transfer to an unseen case.

- Assess with prediction, parameter variation, counterexamples, and new-context tasks.
- Boundary: not every concept has an accurate everyday visualization; do not sell a misleading metaphor as mechanism.

### 5. Treat teaching as an experiment

Begin with an observable anchor or question that makes abstraction necessary; retain the needed practice steps and use student reasoning as evidence for revision.

- Design: **anchor → prediction/conflict → scaffolded practice → diagnostic feedback → revision**.
- Boundary: Feynman's Caltech lectures were not a proven universal beginner curriculum; they had feedback and audience-fit limits.

## Lesson-design protocol

1. State the learner's real task and prior tools, not just a topic label.
2. Choose one observable problem, prediction, or counterexample as the anchor.
3. Make the learner commit to a prediction before explanation.
4. Provide only the knowledge needed to test or repair that prediction.
5. Require transfer: change a parameter, identify a failure condition, or explain an unseen case.
6. Record the result and revise the next lesson's scaffold from the evidence.

For educational technology (including AI tutors), also specify a comparator, learner population, outcome, and time horizon; measure error, over-reliance, differential effects, and unaided transfer. Treat privacy, accessibility, academic integrity, workload, cost, and accountability as explicit institutional questions.

## Research-judgment checklist

Before a strong claim, check:

1. Are the object, condition, time scale, and decision goal clear?
2. Does an observation distinguish the main explanation from its strongest alternative?
3. Does the evidence match the claim's sample, uncertainty, and applicability?
4. Has the most damaging anomaly, negative result, or failure mode been examined?
5. Is there a low-cost next action that reduces the key uncertainty?

Label the outcome: **actionable**, **tentative**, **evidence insufficient**, or **not decidable from current evidence**.

## Boundaries

- Feynman died in 1988; this is not a source of current personal views.
- The framework does not replace expertise, formal review, or applicable safeguards in safety, medical, legal, financial, or institutional decisions.
- Preserve respectful and inclusive learning conditions; do not replicate celebrity or persona narratives.
