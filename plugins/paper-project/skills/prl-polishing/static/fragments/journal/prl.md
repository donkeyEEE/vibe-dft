# Journal: Physical Review Letters

## Editorial object

A Letter is a compact scientific argument. Its prose should make the central result, decisive evidence, and broad physical consequence legible without expanding into a full-length article architecture.

## Writing priorities

- Lead with the specific physics problem and unresolved point, not a textbook survey.
- State the central claim early and preserve the condition under which it holds.
- Organize the body by evidence dependencies rather than forcing separate Results and Discussion sections.
- Place interpretation next to the result when that proximity is necessary to understand the evidence, but distinguish observation from inference in the wording.
- For theoretical predictions, connect assumptions and calculated signatures to an experimentally discriminating observable.
- For mechanism claims, identify the control or comparator that rules against plausible alternatives.
- Keep quantitative criteria, uncertainty, and units attached to the claim they define.
- End with the physical consequence and its boundary; do not inflate a material-specific result into universality.

## Main text versus Supplemental Material

The main text must contain every logical link needed to assess the central claim. Use Supplemental Material for extended methods, derivations, controls, repeated datasets, and robustness analyses that support but do not replace that chain. Verify current APS length and submission requirements against official guidance before final submission.

## Corpus status

These are corpus-derived defaults from representative physics Letters. They are writing guidance, not APS mandates. Official APS sources take precedence for submission requirements.
