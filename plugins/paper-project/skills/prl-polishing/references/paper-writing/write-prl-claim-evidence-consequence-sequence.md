# PRL claim–evidence–consequence sequence

A compact PRL argument should introduce a specific scientific claim, present the decisive evidence or calculation that tests it, and state the physical or experimental consequence before moving to a secondary point.

## Use

Use this sequence to test whether an abstract, introduction, results paragraph, or figure order answers the reader's implicit questions: what is newly claimed, what establishes it, and why does it matter. The consequence may be a measurement signature, a device function, or a constraint on interpretation; it need not be an application claim.

## Caveats

This is a corpus-derived writing pattern, not an APS requirement. It does not require every paragraph to repeat all three moves, and it must not turn an unresolved inference into a stated consequence.
