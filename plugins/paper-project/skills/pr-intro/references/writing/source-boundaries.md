# Source Boundaries

## Input precedence

Treat the user's draft, research material, and confirmed corrections as the
primary source. Build the available-facts view from that material before
considering retrieval. Keep conflicts and missing information visible.

Retrieve literature only when the user has explicitly permitted it. Use the
available Zotero or research capability rather than duplicating its workflow.
Add retrieved background to the available-facts view with a traceable source
before using it in prose.

## Claim boundaries

Claims of novelty, generality, mechanism, or an exhaustive research gap require
direct support. When support is absent or ambiguous, narrow the claim or report
the missing evidence. Distinguish a source's statement from an inference, and
retain the conditions or scope that make a statement defensible.

When supplied facts establish an association or say that symmetry or another
factor controls an effect, that relation may support a bounded question or
classification. It does not by itself establish a microscopic mechanism,
atomistic foundation, causal sufficiency, or complete selection principle.

If the visible context supplies only one opening premise and no fact packet,
build the next step from its stated entities and relations. A newly named
theory, mechanism, material class, domain structure, measurement, or application
is unsupported even when it is familiar background; it may appear only after
the user supplies or authorizes evidence for it.

Use confirmed methods, findings, and conditions only. Do not infer a study's
result from its topic, fill a rhetorical move with plausible scientific
content, or turn a literature search result into an established fact without
checking the source. Ask for the missing material when it blocks a defensible
Introduction.
